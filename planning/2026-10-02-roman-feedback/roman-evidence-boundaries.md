# Roman concrete: evidence boundaries for the W3 sketch

Owner: `roman_evidence`. Scope: primary-source verification and safe labels only. No Figma edits or production. Root owns shared plan/progress files.

## Decision

The Colosseum can anchor the composition as a surviving Roman building, but the sketch cannot treat all four mechanisms as sampled or proven Colosseum facts. The inspected primary studies concern other places or modern test material. Spatial connectors from a cutaway ordinarily imply that the detail occurs at that exact part; reserve these for verified Colosseum structure and restoration. Give material mechanisms their own specimen/process identity.

## Evidence subpanel attribution

- **Harbor/breakwater shard → point 1:** label **로마 항만의 해양 콘크리트 시료**. Jackson et al. examined Baianus Sinus and Portus Neronis submarine breakwaters and a Portus Cosanus subaerial pier. Use an exact specimen/site name only when the reproduced image's provenance identifies it. [Jackson 2017](https://msaweb.org/MSA/AmMin/TOC/2017/open_access/AM102P1435.pdf)
- **Pompeii worksite → point 2:** label **폼페이 Regio IX 공사장 — 서기 79년의 배합 흔적**. The eruption date is the site's archaeological context, not the publication date. The canonical article was published **9 December 2025**, Nature Communications **16, 10847**, DOI **10.1038/s41467-025-66634-7**. It finds dry premixing of quicklime and pozzolan and later calcium-driven remodeling. This supports technique and long-term material evolution at Pompeii. [Vaserman 2025](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-025-66634-7)
- **Privernum lime-clast specimen → points 2 and 4:** label **프리베르눔 성벽 모르타르 — 잔존 석회 입자**. It provides ancient compositional/microstructural evidence and supports the proposed mechanism. Do not label Seymour's Fig. 5 healed crack as an ancient Privernum crack. [Seymour 2023](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9821858/)
- **Modern recreated specimen → point 4:** label **현대 재현 실험 — 약 0.5 mm 균열의 자기치유**. Seymour's **Fig. 5 is explicitly a modern mortar experiment**: hot-mixed Roman-inspired samples were fractured and re-mated with a 0.5 ± 0.1 mm gap. After a 30-day water-flow test, the lime-clast specimen stopped transmitting water and its crack contained precipitated calcite. This is controlled modern evidence, not a time-lapse of the Colosseum. [Seymour 2023](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9821858/)

## Structural and restoration scope

The official Colosseum construction record describes travertine bases approximately 0.90 m high supporting pillars/vaults, and approximately 40 cm lime–red-pozzolana platforms between load-bearing structures. These are documented details of the lower construction system. The same record states that some original bedding materials are delicate and need continuing maintenance when exposed; avoid saying every surviving component became stronger with age. [Official excavation and construction record](https://colosseo.it/en/southern-ambulatories/)

Official restoration history identifies Stern's eastern support and a western brick buttress with a travertine base associated with Valadier's nineteenth-century intervention. An official page gives a broad 1806–1820 phase for the Stern works and associates western stabilization with commissions beginning in 1823; the construction page names Valadier's 1826 restoration. **19세기 보강** is sufficient and avoids presenting a commission date as a completion date. [Official restoration history](https://colosseo.it/percorsi/il-colosseo-romano-e-cristiano/), [Official construction record](https://colosseo.it/en/southern-ambulatories/)

## Pantheon boundary and point 5

Mark & Hutchinson's original structural study concerns the **Pantheon dome**, including gradated lightweight aggregate. This directly supports the identity of that comparison. It does not verify the sketch's Colosseum material sequence, and a publisher abstract alone does not justify precise aggregate densities or layer recipes. Safe action: keep point 3 focused on the Colosseum's pillars, vaults, and foundation; retain grading only as an explicitly labeled Pantheon comparison. [Original publisher record](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/abs/10.1080/00043079.1986.10788309)

Point **5, approximately 1,950 years**, is an **outcome/time context**, not another mechanism or a fifth microscopic detail. A rounded age should not be translated into an uninterrupted performance test or an absence of loss and repair. Give it a time/remaining-structure role in the hierarchy rather than a material-mechanism connector.

## Six-source sufficiency and retrieval limits

Six primary sources are sufficient for the decisions above: three original materials papers, two official Colosseum records, and the original Pantheon structural paper. No broader research is needed for this proposal. Scientific paper bodies were retrieved read-only using Python after browser difficulties. Official Colosseo claims were read in indexed official-page search results; direct retrieval timed out. The Pantheon publisher abstract was readable in search; direct publisher retrieval returned 403 and an archival PDF failed TLS verification. Therefore no density numbers or exact pumice-layer sequence is asserted from that paper. No external write, Figma mutation, or nested delegation occurred.
