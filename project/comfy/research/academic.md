## 1. Design-principle research (infographics / narrative visualization)

### 1.1 Reading order, flow, attention
- **Lu, Wang, Lanir, Zhao, Pfister, Cohen-Or, Huang — "Exploring Visual Information Flows in Infographics"**, CHI 2020. DOI 10.1145/3313831.3376263. https://vcg.seas.harvard.edu/publications/20200301-exploring-visual-information-flows-in-infographics [V]
  - 13K+ infographics (InfoVIF); VIF = backbone traced from visual groups using Gestalt cues (similarity, proximity, regularity, shortest path). [V — read from PDF]
  - **12 VIF patterns** = backbone shape x content placement: landscape, portrait, clock, star, bowl, dome, left-wing, right-wing, up-ladder, down-ladder, pulse, spiral. In 619 manually coded data infographics: spiral 32.3%, clock 21.0%, portrait 19.9%, star 8.4%, landscape 5.8%.
  - *Star* = visual groups spread outside a central object; circular/semi-circular patterns usually put an explicit **theme image at the center**; icons sit closer to the theme than texts. Default reading order assumed when no explicit hint: L->R, top->bottom, **clockwise for radial arrangements**. Regularity (equal segment lengths / turning angles) is what makes a backbone readable.
  - Takeaway for us: our layout is a *star/clock hybrid* (Colosseum = central theme image, ❶–❺ around it). Run ❶->❺ clockwise, keep equal spacing/turning angles along the dashed ring, put icons on the inner side and text on the outer side of the ring, and add arrowheads (an explicit hint) because the default-clockwise assumption is only a fallback.
- **Sultanum & Setlur — "Not Always Top-Left: Untangling the Signals that Guide Dashboard Reading Order"**, TVCG (arXiv 2608.06845, Aug 2026). https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.06845 [V abstract]
  - 18 authors + 16 readers; six factors drive reading order: layout, visual saliency, semantics, functional roles, interaction, user context. Top-left is NOT a universal entry point.
  - Takeaway: on a centered radial map, salience (size/contrast of ❶ and the title) — not position — will set the entry point. Make ❶ the most salient numbered node.
- **Wu & Liu — "An eye-Tracking Study of College Students' Infographic-Reading Processes"**, Journalism & Mass Communication Quarterly 102(3):853–883, 2025. https://scholar.lib.ntnu.edu.tw/en/publications/an-eye-tracking-study-of-college-students-infographic-reading-pro/ [V abstract]
  - 95 students. All readers first fixate the word areas tied to the *most perceptually salient graphic*. High comprehenders show more word<->graph transitions; low comprehenders search text with long saccades.
  - Takeaway: put the label of each CORE step physically adjacent to its most salient illustration (word-graph proximity), keep label->model leader lines short.
- **Bylinskii et al. — "Learning Visual Importance for Graphic Designs and Data Visualizations"**, UIST 2017, https://arxiv.org/abs/1708.02660 ; **Fosco et al. — "Predicting Visual Importance Across Graphic Design Types" (UMSI)**, UIST 2020, https://arxiv.org/abs/2008.02912 , model/data at https://predimportance.mit.edu [V]
  - Neural importance maps trained on human importance annotations for posters/infographics. Code: github.com/diviz-mit/predimportance-public (13 stars, license "other") [V via gh]. UMSI repo: see section 6.
  - Takeaway: run an importance/saliency map on each color iteration and check that the hottest regions = title, ❶..❺ CORE nodes (not decorative buildings).

### 1.2 Memorability, embellishment, pictographs, text
- **Borkin et al. — "What Makes a Visualization Memorable?"** InfoVis 2013 and **"Beyond Memorability: Visualization Recognition and Recall"** InfoVis 2015. https://vcg.seas.harvard.edu/publications/20150101-beyond-memorability-visualization-recognition-and-recall [V]
  - Titles are fixated first and most; a title that states the message improves recall; pictograms/human-recognizable objects do not hurt and can improve recall; redundancy (encode the message twice: text + image) helps.
  - Takeaway: the poster title should state the *answer* (mechanism), not only the question, or have a subtitle that does; repeat each CORE message as both icon/illustration and short text.
- **Harrison, Reinecke, Chang — "Infographic Aesthetics: Designing for the First Impression"**, CHI 2015. https://wildlab.cs.washington.edu/Publications_files/Harrison_CHI2015.pdf [V]
  - 1,278 participants, 500 ms exposure: appeal predicted by colorfulness and visual complexity; colorfulness matters more than complexity; preferences vary by demographics.
  - Takeaway: first-impression appeal is decided by color before reading; an isometric map is already high-complexity, so keep colorfulness moderate-high but complexity managed (limit hue count, large calm background areas).
- **Stokes, Cogley, Satyanarayan, Hearst — "Striking a Balance: Reader Takeaways and Preferences when Integrating Text and Charts"**, IEEE VIS 2022 (TVCG 2023). https://www.tableau.com/research/publications/striking-balance-reader-takeaways-and-preferences-when-integrating-text-and [V]
  - Readers preferred heavily annotated charts; semantic level of text placed in the title vs near the data changes takeaways.
  - Takeaway: put the "why" (relational/causal statement) in the block heading; keep elemental facts (numbers, place names) next to the object.
- **Haroz, Kosara, Franconeri — "ISOTYPE Visualization: Working Memory, Performance, and Engagement with Pictographs"**, CHI 2015 [U — not re-fetched]; **Bateman et al. — "Useful Junk?"**, CHI 2010 [U]. Classic support for pictographic/embellished design not harming comprehension.

### 1.3 Metaphor, genres, map storytelling
- **Guo, Xiao, Shu, Wu, Lee, Liu — "Unpacking Visual Metaphors in Infographics: A Design Space"**, CHI 2026 (Best Paper Honorable Mention). DOI 10.1145/3772318.3790840 [V via search + NCL blog]
  - Systematic review of 2,029 metaphoric infographics; design space = target x source x reconstruction strategy; design-space-augmented prompting of generative models gave more diverse metaphor ideas (n=30).
  - Takeaway: our "open-world game map" is the *source domain*; be explicit about which properties of the source we reuse (path, quest markers, map legend, fog) and do it consistently.
- **Pokojná, Isenberg, Bruckner, Kozlíková, Garrison — "The Language of Infographics: Toward Understanding Conceptual Metaphor Use in Scientific Storytelling"**, IEEE VIS 2024. https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.13416 [V]
  - Ontological and orientational metaphors dominate scientific infographics; proposes taxonomy mapping metaphors to visual components.
  - Takeaway: orientational metaphor "up = strong/enduring, time flows left->right/bottom" — align timeline and "standing 1,950 years" with consistent orientation.
- **Segel & Heer — "Narrative Visualization: Telling Stories with Data"**, InfoVis 2010. https://idl.uw.edu/papers/narrative [V]
  - 7 genres (magazine style, annotated chart, partitioned poster, flow chart, comic strip, slide show, film/video); author-driven vs reader-driven balance.
  - Takeaway: our poster is a hybrid "partitioned poster + flow chart" on a map — a *martini-glass* structure: author-driven CORE ring first, reader-driven DETAIL/SUPPORT exploration second.
- **Roth — "Cartographic Design as Visual Storytelling: Synthesis and Review of Map-Based Narratives, Genres, and Tropes"**, The Cartographic Journal 58(1), 2021 [U — details from search snippet]; **Song, Roth, Houtman, Prestby, Iverson, Gao — "Visual Storytelling with Maps: An Empirical Study..."**, Cartographic Perspectives 100, 2022. https://cartographicperspectives.org/index.php/journal/article/view/1759 [V]
  - Empirical: longform infographic genre + **leader lines** trope improved performance; color highlighting tested as a trope.
  - Takeaway: use leader lines (not just proximity) from SUPPORT pins to their evidence cards; reserve one highlight color for "the story path".
- **Zhang, Xu, Li, Yu, Liu — "MapCraft: Dissecting and Designing Custom Geo-Infographics"**, ChinaVis 2024. https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.13424 [V]
  - 118 geo-infographics -> design space: base map representation, encoding channels, label design & placement, highlighting techniques. Novices improved at labels/highlighting after using the tool.
  - Takeaway: checklist for the map layer: base-map abstraction level, what channel encodes "evidence site" vs "story step", label placement rules, a single highlight technique.
- **Liu, Sreekanth, Patel, Muzumdar, Lopez, Yang, Liu — "What Do We Mean When We Talk About Infographics?"**, IEEE VIS 2026 short paper. https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.12370 [V]
  - Literature (111 papers) + 44 practitioners; disagreement on role of text, relation to data vis, relation to charts/data comics. Useful framing only.
- **Xu, Hu, Shuai, Chen, Yang — "Show Me the Infographic I Imagine: Intent-Aware Infographic Retrieval for Authoring Support"**, arXiv 2604.07989 (Apr 2026). https://infographicretrieval.github.io [V abstract]
  - Intent taxonomy (content + visual design facets) for retrieving exemplar infographics + agent that adapts exemplars. Could complement our 203 benchmark set.

### 1.4 Layout patterns for story infographics (directly matches our radial map)
- **Feng, ..., Chen — "InfoAlign: A Human–AI Co-Creation System for Storytelling with Infographics"**, CHI 2026. DOI 10.1145/3772318.3791844. https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.22901 [V]
  - 70 storytelling infographics -> 6 layouts: Grid (15), **Star — central visual surrounded by semantically linked story pieces (14)**, PortraitGrid (12), Portrait (15), Landscape, **Spiral — pieces along a curved path (12)**. 9 narrative relations (similarity, cause-effect, contrast, violated expectation, temporal, attribution, example, generalization, elaboration). Star/PortraitGrid correlate with "general -> specific"; Spiral with "instance-driven temporal".
  - Takeaway: our poster is a Star (Colosseum hub) whose spokes are ordered like a Spiral/ring. The ring encodes *temporal/causal* order (❶->❺ is cause-effect), so the ring must look like ONE continuous path (single color, constant stroke, arrowheads), while DETAIL blocks hang off it as "elaboration" (visually subordinate: lighter fill, smaller type).

### 1.5 Illustration conventions for magnified/cutaway models
- **Li, Ritter, Agrawala, Curless, Salesin — "Interactive Cutaway Illustrations of Complex 3D Models"**, SIGGRAPH 2007 (ACM TOG 26(3)). https://grail.cs.washington.edu/projects/cutaways [V — conventions read from the PDF]
  - Conventions distilled with scientific illustrators (Netter atlases, Biesty cross-sections):
    1. *Object-aligned box cuts* for man-made / rectangular parts — align the cut to the object's principal axes so viewers infer the missing geometry.
    2. *Tube cuts* (transverse) and *wedge cuts* for cylindrical/radial parts — wedge keeps more exterior context.
    3. *Window cuts* for thin enclosing shells; jagged window edges signal "this was cut" in technical illustration.
    4. *Viewpoint*: center the target, minimize occluders; prefer canonical views.
    5. *Inset cuts*: inner layers cut less than outer ones -> "terraces" that reveal layering.
    6. *Edge shadows*: darken a thin band of the far surface along a depth discontinuity (tighter/darker when surfaces are close) instead of physically accurate cast shadows that hide parts.
    7. *Edge shading*: darken only the edges of cut faces to suggest shading without hiding surface detail.
  - Takeaway: for the magnified isometric "wall section" (lime clasts, volcanic ash matrix, crack + calcite healing) use an object-aligned box cut with terraced inset layers, edge shadows on step edges, and a distinct cut-face color/hatch — this is the scientific-illustration grammar readers recognize.
- **Li, Agrawala, Curless, Salesin — "Automated Generation of Interactive 3D Exploded View Diagrams"**, SIGGRAPH 2008. https://grail.cs.washington.edu/projects/exview3D [V abstract]. Explosion along assembly axes, explosion graphs; useful if the ❶ material step shows ingredients "exploded" out of the mix.
- **Agrawala, Li, Berthouzoz — "Design Principles for Visual Communication"**, CACM 54(4), 2011 [V metadata; full text 403]. Method: identify principles from expert hand-made work -> instantiate -> evaluate. Mechanical explanations should show causal chains with key frames + diagrammatic arrows.

### 1.6 Text, labels, rotation (professor's feedback)
- **Imhof — "Positioning Names on Maps"**, The American Cartographer 2(2), 1975 [V via secondary sources]. Six principles: legible & easy to locate; name<->object association unambiguous; avoid overlap/concealment; names reveal spatial extent/hierarchy; type arrangement reflects classification and hierarchy; **horizontal lettering wherever possible**, follow the feature only for line/area features.
- **Wigdor & Balakrishnan — "Empirical Investigation into the Effect of Orientation on Text Readability in Tabletop Displays"**, ECSCW 2005. https://www.dgp.toronto.edu/public_user/ravin/papers/ecscw2005_textorientation.pdf [V — read]
  - −45°…+45° not significantly different from 0° for reading (≈6–8% slower for single words); at 90° penalties of 26% (words), 54% (phrases), 17% (numbers); Tinker (1972) found >200% for *paragraphs* at 90°. Readers there could move their heads — on a wall poster they cannot rotate it.
  - Takeaway: isometric ±30° place-name labels are acceptable for 1–3 word names; sentences/paragraphs must stay horizontal. Supports the professor's "minimize mixed rotation angles" and our rule "floating text horizontal, only place names on ground axis".
- **Stokes, Arunkumar, Hearst, Padilla — "An Analysis of Text Functions in Information Visualization"**, IEEE VIS 2025. https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.12334 [V]
  - 120 visualizations, 804 text elements -> 10 text functions; 4 strategies: Attribution & Variables, Annotation-Centric, Visual Embellishments, Narrative Framing; text can *replace* visual elements.
  - Takeaway: assign every text block on the poster exactly one primary function (title/narrative frame, step label, mechanism annotation, evidence attribution, legend) and give each function one type style.

### 1.7 Style, color, texture, credibility
- **Song, Cho, Bearfield, Stasko — "Visualizing Trust: How Chart Embellishments Influence Perceptions of Credibility"**, IEEE VIS 2025. https://faculty.cc.gatech.edu/~john.stasko/papers/vis25-credibility.pdf [V via search]
  - Color (vs grayscale) **increased** credibility; hand-drawn fonts, cartoon-style and image-style marks **decreased** it.
  - Takeaway: for a science-explainer poster use a clean/technical illustration style and non-handwritten fonts for evidence/mechanism content; reserve playful "game map" styling for the map frame, not for the evidence cards.
- **Lin, Fortuna, Kulkarni, Stone, Heer — "Selecting Semantically-Resonant Colors for Data Visualization"**, EuroVis 2013 (CGF 32). https://www.tableau.com/research/publications/selecting-semantically-resonant-colors-data-visualization [V] — semantically resonant colors speed up reading vs default palettes.
- **Schloss et al. — Semantic discriminability** ("Semantic Discriminability for Visual Communication", VIS 2020; "Context Matters: A Theory of Semantic Discriminability...", 2021) https://arxiv.org/abs/2108.03685 [V] — colors are interpretable without legends insofar as they are *semantically discriminable* within the set; semantic distance matters more than perceptual distance.
- **Salim, Pial, Mueller — "What is the Color of Serendipity? Investigating the Use of Language Models for Semantically Resonant Color Generation"** (Concept2Color), IEEE VIS 2025. https://aa.ieeevis.org/year/2025/program/paper_b00813fe-c0fb-4bdb-8040-b6e338088247.html [V]
  - Takeaway for us: concept->color candidates: lime/quicklime (warm white), volcanic ash/pozzolana (grey-violet/brown), seawater (blue-green), calcite healing (bright white/cyan accent), fire/hot mixing (orange-red). Check that these hues stay *discriminable from each other* (Schloss) before fixing the palette.
- **Yuan, Zhou, Zhao, Guo, Du, Qu — "InfoColorizer: Interactive Recommendation of Color Palettes for Infographics"**, IEEE TVCG 2021 (VIS 2021). https://arxiv.org/abs/2102.02041 [V] — palettes conditioned on the infographic's element *structure and spatial layout*, learned from designer infographics.
- **Howard & Schloss — "Texture Semantics is Robust to Scaling"**, IEEE VIS 2025 [V abstract] — texture meaning survives zoom-scaling and repeat-scaling (geometric textures). => one texture vocabulary (e.g., aggregate dots for "ash", crystal facets for "calcite") can be reused at map scale and at magnified-model scale.
- **Shi, Wang, Chen, Wang, Lee — "PiCCL: Data-Driven Mark Composition of Bespoke Pictorial Chart"**, VIS 2025 [V title/authors via search]; **"VizBelle: A Design Space of Embellishments for Data Visualization"** (2022; goals: explore data, integrate context/emotion, enhance aesthetics) [U authors/venue].

### 1.9 Practitioner typography heuristics (not peer-reviewed; scale for A2)
- Common poster guides: 3 type levels, each ≥1.6× the one below; A0 conference posters ~80–90 pt title / 34–40 pt headings / 24–28 pt body; cap height ≈ 1 inch per 10 ft viewing distance (signage rule). [V as practitioner claims, not research]
- Our Figma frame 1191×1684 = A2 in **points** (1 unit = 1 pt at print). A2 is half A0's linear size and is read closer (~0.7–1.2 m), so a scaled hierarchy is roughly: title 64–96 pt, CORE step headings 28–36, DETAIL headings 18–22, body 11–13 (Korean min ≈ 10 pt), captions/pins 9–10. [derived heuristic — verify with a 100% print test]
- Hangul fills the em-square while Latin is governed by x-height, so mixed Hangul/Latin/numerals need the Latin set slightly larger or a font family designed for both (e.g., Pretendard, Noto Sans KR/Source Han Sans). [practitioner claim]

## 5. Rules we should adopt (color / illustration / typography stage)

1. **One path, one hue, clockwise.** Draw the CORE ring ❶→❺ as one continuous stroke in a single reserved accent color, with arrowheads, running clockwise from about 12 o'clock with equal spacing between nodes. Nothing else may use that hue. (Lu et al. CHI 2020 on circular VIF, default clockwise reading and regularity; Song & Roth 2022 on color highlighting.)
2. **Entry point by salience, not position.** The title and ❶ get the highest contrast and size. The central Colosseum stays the theme anchor but sits at lower contrast than the CORE nodes, so it does not capture first fixation. (Sultanum & Setlur TVCG 2026; Wu & Liu JMCQ 2025; Lu 2020 on the theme image at the center.)
3. **A semantic palette with a budget.** Use about 5 concept hues (lime, ash/pozzolana, seawater, heat, calcite/healing), each semantically resonant and mutually discriminable, plus one accent for the story path. Everything else (map ground, buildings) stays in desaturated neutrals. (Lin et al. 2013; Schloss et al. semantic discriminability; Harrison et al. 2015 on colorfulness driving first-impression appeal.)
4. **Horizontal text by default.** All sentences, headings and evidence cards stay horizontal. Only short place names (≤3 words) may follow the ±30° isometric ground axes, and no third angle is allowed. (Imhof 1975; Wigdor & Balakrishnan 2005.)
5. **One text function → one type style.** Use these roles: title/narrative frame (states the answer), CORE step label, mechanism annotation, evidence attribution, place name, legend. Each label sits next to the graphic it explains, with short leader lines. (Stokes et al. VIS 2025 on text functions; Borkin 2015 on titles and redundancy; Wu & Liu 2025 on word–graphic proximity; Song et al. 2022 on leader lines.) Suggested A2 scale, derived and to be checked with a print test: title 64–96 pt, CORE headings 28–36, DETAIL headings 18–22, body 11–13 (Korean ≥10), pins and captions 9–10.
6. **Cutaway grammar for close-ups.** Use object-aligned box cuts on wall and pier sections and inset terraces to show layers (mortar, then aggregate, then lime clast, then crack, then calcite). Darken a thin band at step edges (edge shadows) and use distinct edge-shaded cut faces. Avoid realistic cast shadows that hide parts. (Li et al. SIGGRAPH 2007.)
7. **Fidelity by layer, credibility-safe style.** Use low morphological fidelity for the map, and high morphological and cueing fidelity for the magnified models. Use clean vector shading and non-handwritten fonts, and no cartoon styling on evidence content. (Saharan et al. VIS 2026; Song et al. VIS 2025.)
8. **One texture vocabulary across scales.** For example, stipple for volcanic ash, faceted crystal for calcite, smooth fill for lime. Use the same textures on the map and in the magnified models; texture meaning survives scaling. (Howard & Schloss VIS 2025.)
9. **Pictograms are allowed, and redundancy is good.** Each CORE step gets an icon plus a short text, which improves recall without hurting comprehension. Keep decorative elements out of text reading paths. (Borkin 2013/2015; Haroz et al. CHI 2015 [U]; Hu et al. VIS 2026.)

### Upgrades for our blind-judge loop (supervisor + workers + blind VLM judges, 8 criteria, target "AAA")
- **Atomic checklists over holistic grades.** Turn each of the 8 criteria into 3–6 yes/no checks and report a per-question rate plus all-pass. (IGenBench Q-ACC/I-ACC; BizGenEval human-verified binary checklists.) Seed the questions from the AesEval 12 indicators and the PosterIQ dimensions; PosterIQ found MLLMs weakest on visual hierarchy, typographic semantics, saliency control and intention communication.
- **Pairwise against the previous version, order randomized.** MLLM judges agree with humans more in pairwise comparison than in absolute scoring, and they show high-score bias, so a 4/5 means little. (MLLM-as-a-Judge, ICML 2024.)
- **Bounding-box-grounded findings.** Every critique must point to a region. (UICrit and iterative visual prompting; AesEval region selection and localization gains.)
- **Deterministic gates before VLM judges.** Compute from Figma node data: alignment deltas, overlaps, margins, min font size, text rotation ∈ {0°, ±30°}, hue count, contrast ratio. (Haraguchi et al. 2024: GPT judges miss small details; PosterMELD's deterministic gates.)
- **Comprehension quiz.** A judge that has not seen the brief answers about 10 questions on the mechanism from the poster image alone. (Paper2Poster PaperQuiz with verbosity penalty.)
- **Diversity of judges and regression rechecks.** Use judge models from a different family than the generator. After each local fix, re-run earlier passed checks. (Agentic Artifact Creation survey 2026 warns that judges share the generator's blind spots; PosterVisor's recursive contract enforcement; InfoAgent's local repair.)
- **Do not assume reasoning models judge aesthetics better.** (AesEval-Bench.)
- **Saliency sanity check.** Use a UMSI importance map, or a VLM asked "what do you look at first, second, third". (Fosco et al. UIST 2020.)
